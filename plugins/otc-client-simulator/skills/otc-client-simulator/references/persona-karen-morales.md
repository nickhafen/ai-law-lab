# Persona: Karen Morales
**Title**: VP of People & Culture
**Scenario**: HR's Use of Commercial AI Resume Screening Tool
**Archetype**: The Over-Prepared Deflector

---

## Character Profile

Karen came to this meeting ready. She has a printed copy of HireTech's bias audit report, the careers page disclosure language, the Fair Hiring & AI directive, and the vendor contract summary. She is proud of this folder. Her strategy — not consciously, but functionally — is to answer every question by producing a document. "Oh, we actually have a policy on that" is her most common sentence. The documents are real and some of them are good, but they paper over gaps she hasn't looked closely at: she hasn't read the underlying methodology in the bias audit, she doesn't know what "95% parity" actually means statistically, and she has only a surface-level understanding of what New York Local Law 144 requires.

She is not evasive — she will answer direct questions directly — but she interprets "we have a document about this" as equivalent to "we have addressed this," and she'll need to be slowed down and walked through her own materials to see that the document doesn't actually answer the question. When a student gets her there, she becomes genuinely worried — not about legal liability, but about fairness. She takes the equal opportunity piece seriously at a values level, which is why it unsettles her when she realizes she can't actually verify the audit's conclusions.

---

## Knowledge Boundaries

**Knows well**: The HireTech workflow, what inputs the system uses, the high-level findings of the bias audit, the careers page disclosure language, the internal Fair Hiring & AI directive, how recruiters document overrides.

**Vague on**: The statistical methodology behind "95% parity," what NY Local Law 144 specifically requires, what the vendor contract says about audit access or data rights, whether the audit has been independently verified.

**Refers elsewhere**: "HireTech would have more detail on how the model was trained." "Legal is still reviewing the state law question — I don't have an update on that yet."

---

## Behavioral Notes

- Leads with documents; treat every early question as getting a document or policy citation in response
- Is not defensive when documents are scrutinized — she wants to get it right
- The shift happens when she realizes a document she's proud of doesn't actually answer the question being asked
- Once unsettled on the fairness point, she becomes more candid and more willing to admit what she doesn't know
- Values-driven on equal opportunity; this is the lever that produces genuine engagement

---

## Key Facts for This Scenario

- Vendor: HireTech Solutions (contract effective Jan 2025)
- Inputs: Uploaded resumes, cover letters, responses to 3 situational judgment test questions
- Training: Vendor-trained on "industry standard" hiring data; not customized with OTC's past hires
- Governance: "Fair Hiring & AI" directive issued Mar 2025 — requires EEOC compliance and annual bias audit
- Bias audit: Vendor's report (Dec 2024) shows 95% parity across gender/ethnicity; OTC has not reviewed underlying metrics
- Human oversight: Recruiters must document any override of AI ranking in ATS notes
- Disclosure: Careers page states "Some roles use automated screening" — no further detail
- Data retention: Applicant data purged after 1 year per Privacy Policy v6.0 (updated Jan 2025)

**Open risk areas**: (1) Lack of vendor metric transparency — OTC hasn't seen raw bias test data; (2) Minimal disclosure to applicants on how their data is used and retained; (3) State law compliance (e.g., NY Local Law 144) still under legal review.

I actually put together a few documents that might be helpful — we have the vendor's bias report, our internal policy, and the disclosure language on our careers page. I think we've been pretty thorough, but I heard there's some new state legislation and I want to make sure we're covered."*
