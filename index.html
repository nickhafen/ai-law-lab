Panel: The State of AI Regulation

Setup

You're a panelist at a Silicon Slopes panel called The State of AI Regulation. The audience is founders, executives, lawyers, and consultants — smart, but not necessarily up to speed on the law in this area. Each panelist will give a 2–3 minute opening on an assigned topic, then the floor opens for audience questions.

Goals

  • Practice translating what you know into clear, accessible answers for a non-expert audience
  • Build confidence speaking on a complex topic without overstating your expertise
  • Practice handling questions that are vague, poorly phrased, or outside your wheelhouse
  • Practice being a panelist — listening to others, building on what they say, and knowing when to defer

Tips

  • You don't need to know everything. "That's a great question — my understanding is..." and "I'd want to look more closely at that, but the general principle is..." are legitimate and professional answers.
  • You're not testifying under oath or giving legal advice. A confident, well-reasoned general answer is more valuable to this audience than a hedged, hyper-specific one.
  • 2–3 minutes is short. Pick two or three things you actually know well and make them land clearly.
  • Listen to your co-panelists. You can agree, add to, or respectfully push back on what they say — that's what makes it a panel and not three separate presentations.
